# UI pass — design notes (2026-09-26)

Brief: lay the holdings out like Robinhood, and make the UI good.

## Direction

Robinhood's structure, with a data-terminal typographic voice (IBM Plex Mono
figures) that suits a teaching tool where the numbers are the lesson.

Order: portfolio value -> the chart it moves -> the clock that moves it ->
holdings -> watchlist -> history. Holdings lead the watchlist because once you
own something that is the thing you came to check.

Rows, not cards. Hairline dividers and whitespace instead of boxes.

## Signature

The portfolio chart, which extends every time you advance a day, with the S&P
500 drawn beside it in a recessive neutral. Two series, one dollar scale: your
portfolio, and what the same starting cash would be worth in the index. That
comparison is the core lesson of the whole app, so it earns the hero slot.

Cut the old gauge bar: a real chart makes it redundant.

## What rendering caught that tests could not

- The baseline hairline was unlabelled, so it meant nothing; labelled
  "$10,000 start", with a background to mask the lines it overlaps.
- "−$372.00 locked in" broke as "locked / in" on a phone; facts now wrap as
  units via `.seg`.
- Dates without years are ambiguous in a replay spanning 2025 -> 2026.
- An apparent right-edge overflow turned out to be headless Chrome clamping the
  window to 500px and cropping — measured `scrollWidth === clientWidth` rather
  than guessing at a fix.

## Colour

Validated with the dataviz validator, not by eye. Full rationale in CLAUDE.md;
the short version is that the darker greens that satisfy the lightness band lose
colourblind separation, so the brighter #5FCB9B wins for 2px lines on near-black.

## Navigation pass (2026-09-26, later)

Question raised: only "next day"/"next week" existed, with no way back and no
way to reach the present.

**Forward-only is kept, deliberately.** Rewinding lets you see a fall, go back,
and sell before it, which turns every decision into hindsight. The page now says
so (`clockNote`) instead of leaving it looking like a missing feature. Reviewing
the past is served by the chart crosshair and History.

**Reaching the present was a real gap** — a year at 5 days a click is ~50
clicks. Added `+1 month` and `Skip to the end`.

**1D/1M/1Y/5Y ranges went on a stock detail view, not the portfolio chart.**
Portfolio history is only ever as long as the replay so far, so 1Y and 5Y would
be the same picture there. On an individual stock they are useful, so tapping
any ticker now opens its own chart with 1M / 6M / 1Y / Max. No 1D: the data is
daily closes, so a single day is two points.

History extended to 5 years (1272 days, 117 tickers, ~900KB file) so those
ranges have something to show. START moved to 2025-09-24 on the new calendar —
the same replay start date as before, now with four years of lookback.
